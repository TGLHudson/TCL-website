## 1. Head of health, safety and environment, offshore drilling contractor

**First impression.** It's a book that says "stop climbing, fit your culture to your risk", and it comes with a consulting arm. The opening question, "what culture does the work you actually run require, and how far are you from it?", is the first culture pitch in years that hasn't told me to aim for Generative everywhere. No buzzwords, which matters a lot to me.

**What works**
- Home, "A deepwater rig cannot survive a Reactive one. Fit points both ways." That's my world in one line. It also lets me argue for not gold-plating the shore base.
- Home and Consulting, the assessment is "read from the structures in place rather than from a survey score". I've sat through too many questionnaire-led programmes, so this is the hook for me.
- Papers: real SPE work on barriers, bowties, non-compliance and incident distributions, all with DOIs. That counts for more with me than any credential would. The Hudson and Reason provenance is stated openly, so I don't feel misled.

**What I would change** (most important first)
1. **Consulting, "Culture fit assessment".** It doesn't tell me how this sits alongside Hearts and Minds, which my rigs already use. Will you throw out our existing tool results, or build on them? Fix: add one sentence on how the assessment uses, or differs from, existing Hearts and Minds work.
2. **Consulting, "How engagements run".** Four steps, but nothing on the things I'd have to explain to my management: how long it takes, how many days at the work, and who on the rig gets interviewed (crews, contractors, night shift, or only supervisors). Fix: give an illustrative scope once Tim confirms it.
3. **Consulting, multi-site.** I have two rigs and a shore base with different risk spaces. The "unit by unit" footprint is exactly what I need, but it's buried. Fix: put a line near the top saying the footprint compares units.
4. **Consulting, "What we can deliver" and "Talk about your operation".** "We" isn't defined, and I need to know whether Tim himself turns up. Fix: say who delivers the work. Pro-worker and pro-union is stated only on the Author page. Bring it into Consulting with a line on workforce and safety representatives being part of the assessment.
5. **Home, "Pre-order the book".** The button leads to a placeholder. Until CRC Press links exist, change the label and point it at the mailing list.
6. **Book, "Who it is for".** The book itself gives me nothing to read before February. Fix: link one or two papers as "start here", for example SPE-220464-MS.

Before launch, the placeholders that matter most to me are the contact address, the contents list, and a sample extract.

**Would I act?** I'd sign up to the mailing list now and read SPE-220464-MS. I'd pre-order once the contents list confirms there's a working chapter on assessing fit. A consulting enquiry depends on points 1 and 2.

## 2. Professor of safety science

**First impression.** This is a book site with a clear thesis: culture should fit the risk an organisation runs, not climb for its own sake. The site also sells consulting. The thesis reads as advocacy. Within ten seconds I see no sign that the ladder's evidence base is contested.

**What works**
- Provenance is declared openly. The father–son relationship is stated in the footer, on /author/ and on /papers/. Westrum is credited on /book/ and /author/.
- The References page is useful. It lists all 330 sources, groups them by link type and has A to Z anchors. It cites the critics: Boskeljon-Horst, Sillem & Dekker (2023), Busch (2017), Le Coze, Hopkins and Guldenmund.
- Papers are grouped by theme, each with a one-line rationale and a DOI link. That works well as a reading list.

**What I would change** (most important first)
- **/book/, "What it has lacked is an account of why it works".** The only evidence offered is adoption. Adoption is not validation, and the book itself cites a 2023 paper finding that ladder-based assessments inversely predict safety outcomes. Fix: add a short paragraph, "The critiques, and how the book answers them".
- **The novelty claim needs placing.** The structural account sits close to Hopkins (2019), *Organising for Safety: How Structure Creates Culture*. The fit argument sits close to Amalberti (2001) and Grote. Fix: name them on /book/ as the work this builds on.
- **Provenance is incomplete.** Parker and Lawrie (Manchester) built and tested the five-level framework (Parker, Lawrie & Hudson, 2006), yet the footer credits only Hudson and Reason. Hearts and Minds was Shell-funded, and that is not declared. Fix: credit Parker and Lawrie, and state the funding. *(The funding point is the reviewer's recollection and was not verified.)*
- **The "25 papers" count conflicts with itself.** Home and /author/ say Tim has "some 25 peer-reviewed papers and chapters". /papers/ says "25 papers in eight themes", but only 15 of those are Tim's, and some are not SPE papers. Most SPE conference papers are accepted on an abstract, so "peer-reviewed" will be challenged.
- **Citation format is inconsistent** in /references/: an empty year ("BP ()"), a stray space in "Le Coze, J. -C.", inconsistent initials (Dekker; Reason), mixed title and sentence case, hyphens against en dashes in page ranges, "67(3–4)" beside "44 (6)", and SPE numbers in two formats. All records are still `verified: false`.
- **Some "no free copy" labels may be wrong** (from the reviewer's memory, unchecked): Fleming's report on the HSE site, the Baker Panel report on the CSB site, the Buncefield report on the HSE site. /book/ said References holds sources "with a DOI"; it lists all of them. For students, add per-entry anchors.

**Would I act?** I would set /references/ as a bibliography once it is tidied. I would not yet set the site as reading, because it presents the ladder without its critics. A visible engagement with Boskeljon-Horst et al. and Hopkins would get me to pre-order and assign the book.

## 3. Non-executive director, chair of a safety committee (phone)

**First impression.** A serious book, out from CRC Press in February 2027, that swaps "how mature is our safety culture?" for "is our culture right for the risk we run?" That question is the one my committee ought to be asking. What I couldn't tell in ten seconds is whether the book or the author would help a board in particular.

**What works**
- Home hero: the claim comes first, with no hype. A sentence I could repeat in a committee meeting.
- "A village bakery does not need a Generative culture. A deepwater rig cannot survive a Reactive one." Even a non-specialist gets the point at once.
- The Consulting page's "Board briefings" entry, "how to tell a genuine answer from a performative one", is exactly what I'm after. It's just hard to find.

**What I would change** (most important first)
1. **Home, "Four questions".** Written for an operator, and "risk space" is never defined. Fix: frame them as questions a board puts to executives, and define "risk space" once.
2. **Book page, "Who it is for".** The chapter addressed to the board, the workforce and the regulator is buried in the last sentence. For me that is the reason to buy. Fix: give it its own short panel.
3. **Board briefings are hard to find.** On the phone they sit about 2,000px down the Consulting page. Fix: a link near the top of the home page, and move the service to the top of the list.
4. **Home hero buttons.** "Pre-order the book" is the only boxed button, but pre-orders aren't open yet. Fix: make the enquiry or briefing route the boxed button until then.
5. **Chart on phones.** Labels are about 9px. Fix: bigger labels on small screens, or a simpler version.
6. **Before launch:** the contact address matters most; then a short speaker biography.

**Would I act?** I'd share it with my fellow committee members now. I'd ask about a board briefing once there's a real contact address and the site shows, in a couple of lines, the questions the author would equip my board to ask.

## 4. Marketing manager, professional publisher

**First impression.** A CRC Press book due in February 2027 that questions how the safety culture ladder is normally read, with a pre-order button and a consulting route. The thesis comes through well. The author and the subject category do not: the H1 is only "The Culture Ladder".

**What works**
- One main action with a quieter second one.
- The mailing list pitch is specific and promises restraint.
- Every page head has its own title, description, canonical address, `og:url` and `lang="en-GB"`; sitemap and robots.txt are in place.

**What I would change** (most important first)
1. **Social sharing has no image.** No `og:image`, `og:site_name` or `twitter:card`; a LinkedIn post will show as a bare text link. Fix: a 1200 × 630 card now, the cover later.
2. **No structured data.** Fix: JSON-LD `Book` on /book/, `Person` on /author/, `og:type` book.
3. **Pre-order button is a dead end.** Fix: label it "Get notified" until pre-orders open, then link straight to CRC Press.
4. **Titles under-use search terms.** Fix: put "safety culture" and the author's name in the home title; use CRC Press's confirmed subtitle when known.
5. **Nothing for talks and media.** Fix: a press section with biographies of 50, 100 and 200 words, a photo, the cover, a book description and a slide-ready chart.
6. **On a phone the cover is below the fold.** Fix: a small cover beside or above the H1 on narrow screens, once the cover arrives.

Launch-day must-haves: cover, ISBN, retailer links, a working list provider with a GDPR privacy notice, and the share image.

**Would I act?** I would share it internally and sign up once the form is live. Fixes 1 to 3 would tip me over.

## 5. National health and safety officer, trade union (energy and construction)

**First impression.** A management book and a consultancy. Nothing in the first ten seconds says who carries the risk, or that workers have a say in it.

**What works**
- "Fit points both ways" and "Height for its own sake is cost and ceremony" argue against culture programmes run as ceremony.
- Proactive defined as "written triggers that hand authority to the people at the work" is close to a right to stop work; "which barriers depend on judgement and who holds it" makes the same point.
- The Papers page lists "Meeting expectations: A new model for a just and fair culture" and "Non-compliance in complex operations", the two topics where blame lands on workers.

**What I would change** (most important first)
1. **"The work is pro-worker and pro-union"** is a bare assertion near the bottom of the Author page. Say what it means in the book, and put a sentence of it on the home page.
2. **The chapter addressed to the workforce and its representatives** is buried. Give it its own short section: what to ask, and what to refuse.
3. **Every service is aimed at leaders and boards.** Add joint management–union sessions or training for representatives, if Tim offers them; otherwise say nothing.
4. **The assessment** does not say whether representatives take part, or whether the workforce sees the report.
5. **Pathological**: add that the floor is set by the risk and owned by whoever controls it, so the ladder cannot become a way to grade workers' attitudes.
6. **Speaking**: add trade union and safety representative audiences, if Tim accepts those invitations.

**Would I act?** Not yet. What would tip me: the workforce chapter described properly, a clear statement that the ladder grades management structures and not workers, and the just and fair culture paper brought forward. The placeholder that matters most is Tim's own paragraph.

## 6. Accessibility and web-quality audit

axe-core was not available, so checks were run by script and by eye (scripts in `screenshots/a11y/`).

**Overall.** Technically clean: no JavaScript needed, one h1 per page, no skipped heading levels, no duplicate IDs, no broken in-page links, lang="en-GB", a working skip link, and a visible 3px gold focus outline.

**What passes.** Contrast on every text pair actually used (body text 16.2:1 on navy, 9.5:1 on teal; cream labels on teal 10.1:1, where teal would be 3.34:1). No horizontal scroll at 1280, 640, 390 or 320px. Light pages (home about 70 KB on first load).

**Defects, ranked by severity**
1. Sign-up forms post to "#" and fail on submit.
2. "About the book" link on the Author page told apart by colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1).
3. A to Z letters on References below the 24px target size (WCAG 2.5.8).
4. The chart's band at 1.43:1, below 3:1 for meaningful graphics (WCAG 1.4.11).
5. Chart labels shrink to 6 to 8px on phones; the text alternative leaves out three labels.
6. One citation overflows at 320px with text spacing applied (WCAG 1.4.12).
7. Ambiguous link names: repeated "Free copy at …" and "Request a copy" labels (AAA 2.4.9).
8. Advisories: font sizes in px; no visible "required" on the email field; footer links not in a `<nav>`.

Fix before launch: items 1 to 4.
